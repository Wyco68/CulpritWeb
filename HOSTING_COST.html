3The three decisions we need from you Each one states what we propose, what it risks, and what the alternative costs.

Decision 1  ·  Run the database on the free plan, with no automatic backups?

The free Supabase plan holds all the site's content and booking records at no cost, but it takes no automatic backups. If the data were ever lost, there would be no copy to restore from.

What that means in practice. One person, the admin, enters all of the content. Recovering from a loss means that person re-typing the bio, research entries, publications, groups and events. The only thing that could not be re-created is the record of past bookings. The chance is low, and it drops further with a manual export now and then.

We propose: stay free. If you would rather not: Supabase Pro adds daily backups kept for seven days, at $25 a month (฿828), and can be switched on at any time without changing the site.
Decision 2  ·  Buy one domain, used for both the web address and the email?

A single domain at about $12 a year (฿397) does two jobs: it is the address visitors type, and it is the "from" address on the emails the site itself sends.

Why the email side matters. The site's own email is short but critical: the verification code the admin needs to log in. Resend's free plan allows 3,000 emails a month tied to one domain per account, so any sending account without its own domain has to share that allowance. On a shared account a busy day elsewhere can exhaust it, this site's emails stop being delivered, and the admin cannot get into the admin panel. A domain of our own gets its own registration, its own key and its own 3,000 emails.

We propose: buy one domain. If you would rather not: the site can run free on culprit.wyco-dev.com, a sub-address of a domain already owned, and the email would need a second free Resend account registered against it. That works and costs nothing, but it is two accounts to keep track of instead of one domain.
Decision 3  ·  Store the photos and PDFs on Cloudflare R2 rather than Supabase?

Both are free. R2 gives ten times the space and, crucially, never charges for sending files to visitors, so the site's ceiling rises from about 650 visitors a day to several thousand. Section 7 has the comparison in full.

We propose: use R2. Cost: $0 in fees, one new storage module in developer time, because the application already keeps file storage behind a swappable interface. If you would rather not: Supabase works today and the limit only bites above roughly 650 visitors a day, at which point the fix is $25 a month (฿828).

6How the appointment system works Confirmed against the built application, because it decides what has to be paid for and what has to be protected.

  1. The visitor books inside Calendly. The "Make Appointment" tab shows Calendly's own booking calendar embedded in the page. There is no separate request form and no approval step. The booking is made and confirmed by Calendly directly.
  2. Calendly handles all the booking email. The confirmation to the visitor, the notice to the professor, reminders and cancellation notices all come from Calendly. Our site sends none of it, which is why the appointment flow costs nothing in email.
  3. A copy of the booking is recorded in our database. This is what the admin panel reads, and what the public "Upcoming Events" tab draws from.
  4. The admin decides what the public sees. Settings has a switch for the Upcoming Events tab. On, future bookings appear publicly. Off, nothing is shown. It is off by default.
  5. Cancellations flow back. A cancelled booking is marked cancelled rather than deleted, so the history stays intact.
What the free Calendly plan means for step 3

The automatic copying of a booking into our database is a paid Calendly feature. It sits on Calendly Standard at $10 a month (฿331), not on the free plan.

On the free plan the admin types each booking into the admin panel by hand. Calendly emails the admin whenever someone books, so nothing is missed. It is one short form per booking, at roughly thirty bookings a month. Everything downstream, the admin table, the Upcoming Events tab, the cancellation history, works exactly the same once the entry is made.

Switching on the automatic version later is a subscription and a settings change, not a rebuild. It is priced as an optional line in section 9.

Proposed addition · costs nothing

Put a Cloudflare Turnstile check in front of the booking calendar. Right now the Calendly widget loads for anyone who opens the page, including automated scripts. Calendly's free plan has no abuse protection of its own, and it allows only one appointment type, so a bot making fake bookings would fill the professor's real calendar and block genuine visitors.

Turnstile shows a quick "are you human?" check, and the booking calendar only loads once it passes. Most visitors see nothing more than a brief tick. The same service is already planned for the admin login, so this uses the setup twice.

Cost: $0. Cloudflare Turnstile is free for all accounts. The work is a small change to one page.

What our own email is for, then. Not bookings. Resend carries the verification code the admin needs to sign in, and any future notice the site itself generates. That is a small volume but a critical one: if it fails, nobody can reach the admin panel. It is the reason Decision 2 matters more than its $12 price suggests.

Why we recommend it

Put the photos and PDFs on Cloudflare R2 and keep the database on Supabase. Each supplier then does the thing it is best at, both stay free, and the site's ceiling stops depending on how many people look at the professor's photograph.

The practical difference: on Supabase the site is comfortable to about 650 visitors a day; on R2 the limit moves to the server itself, several thousand a day. Against an expectation of 100 a day, that is the difference between six times headroom and fifty.

It also settles the database question. With the download limit gone, the free Supabase plan comfortably covers everything this site stores, so there is no case for running and maintaining a database ourselves.

How the visitor capacity figures were worked out

  • One visit is assumed to be three pages, about thirty requests, and roughly 250 KB of files downloaded, falling to about 100 KB once Cloudflare caches repeat images.
  • Supabase 5 GB ÷ 250 KB ≈ 20,000 visits a month ≈ 650 a day. With caching, ≈ 1,650 a day.
  • Cloudflare R2 has no download charge at all, so no visitor limit follows from it. The 10 GB storage allowance is about space, not traffic.
  • Vercel 1,000,000 requests ÷ 30 per visit ≈ 33,000 visits a month ≈ 1,100 a day.
  • Server capacity figures are engineering estimates for this application, not supplier guarantees.

Other assumptions
